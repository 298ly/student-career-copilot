# Application operations

## Rank a JD batch

Evaluate hard constraints first. Then compare role direction, evidence coverage, trainable gaps, application cost, deadline, and learning value. Keep requirement-level reasoning visible; do not hide vetoes inside one weighted score. Rank only comparable roles together or label cross-family tradeoffs.

## Prepare an application package

1. Snapshot the exact JD text, URL, access date, company, role, location, deadline, and source.
2. Derive from the relevant role-family resume; do not edit the master evidence profile to mimic the JD.
3. Record every material resume change and its evidence ID.
4. Run a fresh review for truthfulness, relevance, consistency, and interview risk.
5. Compile and visually inspect PDF output. Extract its text with Poppler or a PDF library and confirm contact details, headings, reading order, and supported JD keywords.
6. Ask for explicit authorization before submitting or messaging anyone.

## Archive what was submitted

Create `applications/<application_id>/` containing `manifest.yaml`, the JD snapshot, the submitted resume, optional cover letter, and later interview/outcome records. Never replace the submitted files with a newer version.

## Record outcomes

Separate observed stage changes from inferred causes. Record recruiter wording verbatim only when necessary and brief; otherwise paraphrase. Link each interview question to the resume/JD version that prompted it.

After several comparable outcomes, update role hypotheses and learning priorities. Do not overreact to silence, a single rejection, or a single positive result.

## Chinese student defaults

Prefer a concise Chinese one-page base resume unless the JD or employer requests another format. Treat Boss, internship, campus-hire, and official company pages as different channels with different deadlines and information quality. Do not assume European or US ATS conventions, cover-letter norms, or two-page CV expectations apply.
