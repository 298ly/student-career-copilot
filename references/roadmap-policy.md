# Adaptive roadmap policy

## Task contract

Every roadmap task must include:

- stable task ID and status;
- role family or JD target;
- source evidence IDs;
- reason and confidence;
- priority `P0` to `P2`;
- start date and deadline;
- observable completion criteria;
- career artifact produced;
- application policy while learning.

## Prioritization

- `P0`: repeated personal failure, a frequent core target-JD requirement, or an immediate application blocker.
- `P1`: important transferable capability or recurring preference with clear portfolio value.
- `P2`: low-frequency or speculative skill; defer unless the target strategy changes.

Prefer the smallest task that changes hiring evidence. Replace “learn Tableau” with “build and explain one dashboard answering a target-role business question.”

## Update threshold

Update immediately for explicit credible feedback or a verified assessment result. Require a repeated comparable pattern before interpreting silence or rejection. Keep alternative explanations visible. Do not abandon a role family after one outcome.

## Balanced execution

Unless a hard blocker applies, include applications during learning. A seven-day plan should allocate time to real applications, resume tailoring, interview drills, and evidence-producing learning rather than only study.

Do not invent a universal application target. Derive volume from available hours, relevant JD supply, tailoring cost, deadlines, and observed response rates. When those inputs are absent, start with a manageable measurement batch and define what to learn from it.
