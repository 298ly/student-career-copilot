# JD application decision rubric

## Parse before judging

Classify each requirement as a hard constraint, core duty, trainable skill, preference, or boilerplate. Check whether wording describes eligibility, daily work, or an ideal candidate.

## Actions

- `apply_now`: no known hard blocker; core duties have credible evidence; current resume already communicates the fit.
- `tailor_then_apply`: no known hard blocker; evidence exists but is hidden, poorly ordered, or expressed in mismatched vocabulary.
- `apply_while_learning`: direction fits; gaps are trainable before a plausible interview and do not make current claims misleading.
- `learn_first`: a real blocker exists or the student cannot yet answer foundational questions implied by their own resume. Specify a short deadline and exit test.
- `skip`: eligibility, constraints, interests, or role substance conflicts materially; or opportunity cost dominates realistic upside.

## Guardrails

Do not convert requirement counts into a universal match score. One hard constraint may outweigh ten preferences. Conversely, an unfamiliar tool may be minor when adjacent evidence is strong.

When evidence is insufficient, state what would change the decision. If the application cost is low and no blocker is known, do not use uncertainty alone to recommend endless preparation.
